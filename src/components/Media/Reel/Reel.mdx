# Reel

A set of square images on a disc, like a View-Master reel. One shows at a time; after a hold, the disc turns: the current image arcs up and out, and the next rises from below. It turns once as the page loads, so someone scanning the page sees it move.

<Canvas of={ReelStories.Default} />

## When to use

- **As a Hero’s media, to show the kind of work a page is about,** as on Home, where five design screenshots back up “Product designer with a creative toolkit.”
- **On a band that clips,** like a Hero with `background="subtle"`, `"knockout"`, or `"brand"`. The cards move past the Reel’s own edges, and the band hides them at its edges.
- **Not for images people need to study or compare.** Each one is gone in a few seconds. Use `Gallery`, or an `Image` in a `MediaBlock` with a caption.
- **Not for content.** The images are decorative; what they show should also be on the page, like the case studies below Home’s Hero.

## Accessibility

A Reel is a group (`role="group"`) named by `label`, “Examples of my design work” by default, so screen readers hear what the images are once. Each image is decorative (`alt=""`), and changing images isn’t announced: there’s no live region.

| Situation | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Naming the group | Say what the images are examples of, in sentence case | “Examples of my design work” |
| Choosing images | Square images of real work, five of them. At least two; with one, there’s nothing to turn. | Five case study screenshots |
| Saying what the work is | In the page, not the images: they have no alt text | The case studies below the Hero |

**Pausing (WCAG 2.2.2).** It moves on its own for more than five seconds, so it has a pause button: an `IconButton` (`md`, `background="scrim"`) with the `pause` icon, “Pause the reel,” which becomes `play`, “Play the reel.” The visitor’s choice sticks. It also holds, without anyone pressing anything:

| While | Why |
|---|---|
| The pointer is over the Reel | To look at an image, or to reach the button |
| Keyboard focus is inside it | So the button stays put while someone tabs to it. Only keyboard focus: pressing the button with a mouse leaves focus on it, and the Reel still plays. |
| It’s off screen | Nobody’s watching |
| The tab is hidden | The same |

A turn that has started finishes, then it holds. When it plays again, the hold picks up where it stopped.

## States

The pause button shows only when it’s needed, by the same rule as Animation’s and Video’s:

| State | The button | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Playing | Hidden (see-through), fading in over `--verity-motion-duration-fast` on hover over the Reel or keyboard focus on the button | Out of the way of the images. Hovering also holds the Reel, so the button appears exactly when it stops. |
| Paused | Always shows, with the `play` icon | So the way to start it again is always there |
| On a touch screen | Always shows | There’s no hover to bring it back |

The cards have no states of their own. A card’s scrim shows only how far it is from its spot (see Motion), not hover or focus.

Hidden only means see-through: keyboard users can still tab to it. The button’s own hover, focus, and press states are `IconButton`’s, with `background="scrim"`.

## Motion

**The entrance.** The Reel opens on its last image, at rest. `--verity-reel-duration-entrance-delay` (600ms) after the page loads, it turns once to the first image, a normal turn, then the holds and turns below continue from there. Motion gets noticed when it starts: a Reel that waited a full hold could pass for a still image to someone scanning the page. The entrance follows the same pausing rules as any turn: if the pointer or keyboard focus is on the Reel, it’s off screen, or the tab is hidden, it waits, and turns once nothing’s holding it.

**Each turn.** Every image holds for `--verity-reel-duration-hold` (2 seconds), then the disc turns, in four phases with no pause between them:

| Phase | Movement | Duration | Easing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance delay | Only before the first turn: the last image rests, after the page loads | `--verity-reel-duration-entrance-delay` (600ms) | None |
| Hold | Every image rests, after each turn | `--verity-reel-duration-hold` (2s) | None |
| Dip | The disc turns back by `--verity-reel-angle-dip` (10°): the current image sinks | `--verity-reel-duration-dip` (340ms) | In and out |
| Rise | Up past the next image’s spot, to the curve’s peak | `--verity-reel-duration-rise` (525ms) | `--verity-reel-easing-rise`, up to its peak only |
| Fall | Back through the spot to `--verity-reel-angle-settle` (3°) below it | `--verity-reel-duration-fall` (300ms) | In and out |
| Settle | Up to rest | `--verity-reel-duration-settle` (260ms) | In and out |

The rise uses only the part of `--verity-reel-easing-rise`, `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.78, 0.64, 1)`, up to its overshoot peak (at 52.5% of its time, 17% past the target), stretched to fill the rise’s duration. The code finds the peak from the curve, so a new curve needs no other change. The curve’s own tail creeps back slowly, which would read as a pause; the fall and settle replace it.

### The disc

The images sit 360° / count apart on a disc (72° for five). Its pivot is `--verity-reel-radius` (1.7) card widths to the right of the resting card’s center, so the cards arc up and to the right, and the next rises from below. A card `d` degrees from its resting spot (positive is past it, up and out) moves `translate(R·(1 − cos d), −R·sin d)`, with R the radius times the card’s width, and turns `d × --verity-reel-tilt` (0.6): 1 would be a rigid disc, 0 would keep the cards upright.

Cards more than 120° from their spot aren’t drawn, and the card nearest its spot is on top.

### The scrim

Each card has a scrim over it: `--verity-color-background-default` at an opacity that follows the card’s distance from its resting spot, the same distance the motion uses. So a card away from its spot reads as further back, and never puts a bright picture behind the text it passes.

| Distance from its spot | Scrim |
|---|---|
| Up to as far as a turn carries a card from its spot and back (13.9° for five cards: the overshoot) | None, so the dip, the overshoot, and the settle stay bright |
| From there to half the spacing between images (36° for five) | Rising smoothly (a smoothstep) |
| Beyond | `--verity-reel-scrim-opacity` (0.7) |

The code works out the clear range from the dip and the overshoot, so new angles or a new curve need no other change.

### Reduce motion

With reduce motion on, nothing moves, there’s no entrance, and there’s no scrim: the Reel opens on the first image. Each turn is a crossfade in place, over `--verity-reel-duration-rise`, with the same hold and the same pausing.

## Size

| Part | Token |
|---|---|
| Each card, square | `--verity-reel-size`, and never wider than its space, so it shrinks with a Hero’s media (down to `--verity-hero-media-min-width` beside the text). It points to `--verity-hero-media-width` (560 on XL, 440 on Large, 260 on Medium, 280 on Small), so a Reel is always the same size as a photo in a Hero. |
| Corners | `--verity-border-radius-md` |
| The pause button, from the bottom and right edges | `--verity-spacing-xs` |

The button stays in the corner while the cards move, and shows when it’s needed (see States). The images are cropped to fill the square (`object-fit: cover`).

## Loading

Before the script runs, the last image shows at rest, from the CSS alone, and that’s what shows without JavaScript. With reduce motion on, it’s the first image instead, since there’s no entrance. Whichever paints first loads right away, ahead of other images (`fetchpriority="high"`), since it’s likely the page’s largest image. The others wait until the page has loaded. Each card shows a `Skeleton` until its image arrives.

## In a Hero

The Reel goes in a Hero’s `media`. It’s the same size as a photo there: `--verity-reel-size` points to the Hero’s `--verity-hero-media-width`. Like a photo, it shrinks with the Hero’s media: beside the text, the media can give up width down to `--verity-hero-media-min-width` (320 on Large) before it wraps, and the Reel fills it, square. The turn scales with it, since its geometry comes from the card’s width, and the pause button stays in the corner. It sits beside the text at every width on XL and Large, from 672px on Medium, and always under it on Small; on a band, from 1116px on Large and 768px on Medium (see Hero’s docs). The Hero keeps the text above it, so the cards pass behind the text and never cover it, and a visible band clips the cards at its edges and rounded corners.

## In Figma

| Figma | Code |
|---|---|
| Component `Reel` | `<Reel />` |
| The Image instance | The first of `images`, at rest, as it is after the entrance. Figma has no variants: it shows the resting state. |
| The Pause button instance | The pause and play button, made by Reel. Figma shows it; in code it’s hidden while the Reel plays, until hover or keyboard focus (see States). |
| `reel/size` | The card’s width and height. It points to `hero/media-width`. |
| Not in Figma | `images` and `label`, the entrance, the turn, pausing, and reduce motion. The tokens marked “Code only” (`reel/angle/*`, `reel/radius`, `reel/tilt`) are used only here. |
